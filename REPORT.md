# Pencil Exchange: behavioral evaluation and failure taxonomy

**Phase 1 (behavioral) — complete.** Harshith, 2026-09-08.

Four open instruction-tuned models (≤9B) read a synthetic pencil-transfer story
and answer questions about the state in strict JSON. We swept `N = T = 2 … 30`,
recorded every generation, and classified every wrong answer by counterfactual
replay against the simulator.

**24,893 scored generations** (19,796 sweep − 1,503 context overflow, + 6,600
ablation) across Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct,
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct.

---

## 1. The three findings

**1. Binding survives; accumulation does not.** `initial_state_lookup` — "how
many pencils did X hold at timestep 0?", answerable by copying one number from
the prompt — stays at **100% at N=12 and 83% at N=30**. Over the same range
`trajectory` and `state_snapshot` fall to **0%**. The models can still bind a
person to a value in a 30-person list while being unable to carry that value
through a single update.

**2. Tracking fails at the first update, not by drift.** Mean
`first_divergence_index` on the `trajectory` question is a flat **1.0–2.0 in
every well-powered cell**, against a perfect-tracking reference of `y = T`.
(Eight cells fall to 0.25–0.80, all of them Qwen2.5 or OLMo-2 at high N where
`degenerate_sequence` exclusions leave only 2–5 diverging answers; they move the
estimate down, never up, so the conclusion is unaffected.) Models reproduce
`t = 0` and then leave the true trajectory immediately. There is no regime in
which they track for a while and gradually decay.

**3. Number word form is irrelevant.** Rendering every number as a numeral
instead of a word (`17` for `seventeen`) changes accuracy by −0.1 to +0.5
points, with every 95% interval excluding an effect larger than ±1.5 points.

Together these rule out tokenisation, number form, and person↔value binding as
the bottleneck, and locate the failure in the **state update** itself.

---

## 2. Accuracy

Accuracy does **not** collapse to zero. It falls to a **~22% floor by N ≈ 12**
and flattens, with all four models indistinguishable past that point.

| N | Qwen2.5 | Llama-3.1 | Mistral | OLMo-2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 62.0% | 55.9% | 57.1% | 50.5% |
| 4 | 43.3% | 43.0% | 42.1% | 37.9% |
| 8 | 33.3% | 31.2% | 34.8% | 25.2% |
| 12 | 34.2% | 29.4% | 30.6% | 28.2% |
| 20 | 31.5% | 26.1% | 24.2% | 22.3% |
| 30 | 22.7% | 23.3% | 18.5% | — |

The floor is an artefact of question mix, not residual skill: it is held up by
`initial_state_lookup` (~100%), `pairwise_comparison` (~40%, two-way choice) and
`transfer_recall`. **The headline figure should not be read as "models retain
22% of the capability."** Figure 2 is the one to show.

OLMo-2's line ends at N=22 because its 4096-token context runs out. Those
prompts are recorded `context_overflow: true`, excluded from every denominator,
and never scored 0%. Cells where overflow removed more than half the questions
are dropped from the figures entirely — at N=24 only 20 of 330 records survive,
and they are the shortest stories and easiest types, a biased subsample rather
than "OLMo-2's accuracy at N=24".

### The dissociation (Qwen2.5 shown; the other three match within a few points)

| question type | what it needs | N=2 | N=8 | N=16 | N=30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `initial_state_lookup` | copy one stated number | 100% | 100% | 100% | **83%** |
| `transfer_recall` | retrieve one narrated event | 93% | 87% | 60% | 33% |
| `pairwise_comparison` | compare two states | 67% | 30% | 37% | 40% |
| `person_timestep_lookup` | one accumulated value | 53% | 23% | 20% | 20% |
| `population_condition` | all states + a threshold | 63% | 37% | 20% | 10% |
| `state_snapshot` | the whole state vector | 67% | 13% | 13% | **0%** |
| `trajectory` | the whole history of one entity | 20% | 13% | 7% | **0%** |
| `person_value_timesteps` | history + a match | 33% | 0% | 0% | **0%** |

The ordering is monotone in how much accumulation the question needs, and it is
the same ordering in all four models.

## 3. Failure taxonomy

Every wrong answer is matched against counterfactual answers generated by
replaying the simulator under one specific hypothesised corruption. All matches
are recorded in `labels_matched`; `label` is the winner by a fixed priority.
Rates are **chance-corrected**: the null draws uniformly random wrong answers
and is measured the same way as the observed rate (a distribution over winning
categories), so the subtraction is meaningful.

N = 8–12, all models pooled, share of mechanistic wrong answers:

| category | observed | chance | corrected |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arithmetic | 15.4% | 3.3% | **+12.1** |
| Omission | 8.5% | 0.5% | **+8.0** |
| Binding | 7.1% | 2.3% | **+4.8** |
| Over-application | 3.2% | 0.6% | +2.6 |
| Degenerate | 2.3% | 0.0% | +2.3 |
| Direction | 2.8% | 0.6% | +2.2 |
| Temporal | 1.4% | 0.4% | +1.0 |
| Digit | 1.7% | 1.5% | +0.3 |
| Unexplained | 30.6% | 30.4% | +0.2 |
| Referent | 16.8% | 23.0% | −6.2 |
| Conservation | 10.6% | 38.7% | −28.1 |

### Three things this table says that a raw taxonomy would have hidden

**`Unexplained` is the largest observed category, and it is exactly chance.**
30.6% observed against 30.4% expected: about a third of wrong answers are
statistically indistinguishable from a random integer in range. The 9-category
taxonomy inherited from `Papers.md` §7 does not fit those answers. That is a
result to report, not a gap to paper over — and it caps how much of the failure
distribution any mechanistic story can claim to explain.

**`Conservation` and `Referent` fire far below chance** (−28.1, −6.2). The
uniform-integer null over-fires badly for them, so their corrected values are
not interpretable and they are omitted from figure 3 and reported observed-only.
Reporting −28% as a "rate" would be meaningless.

**The interpretable positives are Arithmetic, Omission and Binding**, in that
order. Note that Binding at +4.8 is real but small — consistent with finding 1,
where the pure binding control is at ceiling.

Format errors are 2.7% in this regime and are never assigned a mechanism.
Taxonomy ambiguity (more than one label matched) is 19.4%; chance coverage —
the share of the cell for which a null is defined — is 0.74.

## 4. The digits ablation

`number_word_probability = 0.0`, N ∈ {6,10,14,18,22}, 1,650 prompts per model,
paired on `question_id` with the main sweep (identical seeds and sampler; only
number rendering differs), so this is McNemar on discordant pairs.

| model | words | digits | delta | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-7B | 29.5% | 29.4% | −0.1% | [−1.1%, +1.0%] | 1.000 |
| Llama-3.1-8B | 28.3% | 28.4% | +0.1% | [−0.9%, +1.1%] | 0.903 |
| Mistral-7B-v0.3 | 25.8% | 26.3% | +0.5% | [−0.4%, +1.5%] | 0.313 |
| OLMo-2-7B | 26.6% | 26.9% | +0.3% | [−0.7%, +1.4%] | 0.590 |

A **well-powered null**: every interval excludes an effect larger than ±1.5
points. Reported as an interval rather than a p-value, because a null is only
informative if it says what it rules out.

This is the one clean manipulation in the design — `pencils_per_person` and
narration order are deliberately held fixed — so it isolates surface number
format, and finds nothing.

## 5. Against the five hypotheses

| | hypothesis | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Binding** — larger N corrupts person↔value association | **Largely rejected.** `initial_state_lookup` is at 100% (N=12) and 83% (N=30). Binding errors are real but small (+4.8). |
| H2 | **Retrieval** — transfers looked up at query time | **Partly supported.** `transfer_recall` degrades 93%→33%, so retrieving a narrated event does get harder — but it stays far above the accumulation questions, so retrieval alone cannot explain them. |
| H3 | **State update** — the running state drifts | **Supported, with one correction: it does not drift, it fails at once.** First divergence is flat at 1–2 for every T. |
| H4 | **Arithmetic** | **Supported as the largest interpretable component** (+12.1), but the digits ablation shows it is not about number form. |
| H5 | **Readout** | **Not testable here.** `pairwise_comparison` (~40%) does stay above the numeric questions, which is consistent with H5, but consistent is not confirmed — this needs probing. Do not claim it. |

The behavioral phase cannot confirm any mechanism; it narrows which one is worth
GPU-weeks. On this evidence that is **the state-update step**.

---

## 6. Limitations — state these, do not smooth them over

- **N confounds tracking with arithmetic.** `pencils_per_person = 5` is fixed,
  so N=30 puts 150 pencils in play and individual counts reach ~31. Arithmetic
  difficulty grows with N independently of tracking difficulty. Deliberate
  choice; a fixed total would separate them and is the obvious follow-up.
- **The story is not narrated in chronological order.** `execution_schedule`
  interleaves clusters, so a `t=1` paragraph can appear after a `t=7` one — at
  N=25 the paragraph order runs `1,1,1,2,3,4,2,2,3,3,4,5,4,5,6,7,1,8,…`. The
  task therefore measures tracking **plus narrative reordering**. Deliberate
  choice; it is not a clean manipulation of N.
- **~30% of wrong answers are unexplained at chance level** (§3).
- **H5 is untestable behaviorally** and must not be asserted.
- Greedy decoding is not bit-identical across batch size or tensor-parallel
  settings. `max_num_seqs=64`, `tensor_parallel_size=1` were fixed for the whole
  sweep and are recorded in every record.

## 7. Regime for the mechanistic phase: **N = T = 8–12**

3,960 records, 30.3% accuracy, and every property the next phase needs:

| criterion | value | why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| accuracy | **30.3%** (27.3–32.4% across models) | a real mix of successes and failures to contrast; not a ceiling or a floor |
| prompt length | **1,090 tokens mean, 2,109 max** | short enough for activation patching and full attention-map capture; fits every model including OLMo-2's 4096 |
| format errors | **2.7%** | almost nothing discarded before analysis |
| taxonomy ambiguity | **19.4%** | most wrong answers get a single label |
| the dissociation | fully developed | `initial_state_lookup` 100%, `transfer_recall` 70.8%, `trajectory` 2.8% |
| all four models | comparable | 27–32%, so cross-model circuit comparison is meaningful |

The dissociation is already complete at N=8–12, so nothing is gained by paying
for N=30 — where prompts are 3× longer, OLMo-2 is absent entirely, and every
model is at the floor.

**The specific target:** the first state update. `initial_state_lookup` at 100%
and first divergence at index 1 localise the failure to the transition from the
stated `t=0` state to `t=1`. The contrast to run is a correct
`initial_state_lookup` against a wrong `person_timestep_lookup` at `t=1` for the
same person in the same story — the prompts differ minimally and the model
succeeds on one and fails on the other.

## 8. Reproducing this

```bash
export PROJ=/home/cccp/25m0834/MTP_Mechanistic_Interpretability && cd $PROJ
source envs/eval/bin/activate

sbatch scripts/run_eval.sbatch          # sweep,    ~13 min/model
sbatch scripts/run_ablation.sbatch      # ablation, ~10 min/model

python -m src.regrade  --run-dir results/raw/<run> --out-dir results/raw/<run>_regraded
python -m src.aggregate --run-dir results/raw/<run>_regraded
python -m src.plots
python -m src.compare_ablation --words-run <sweep>_regraded --digits-run <ablation>_regraded
```

`src/regrade.py` rebuilds every graded field from the stored `raw_output` in
about five seconds. Three grading bugs were fixed this way with no GPU time; the
largest had Mistral scored at 9.4% when it is 28.4%, because it ends most
answers one `}` short. Keep storing raw text.

**Artifacts.** Sweep `results/raw/2026-09-07T1341Z_304971/` (raw, append-only)
and `…_304971_regraded/` (graded). Ablation
`results/raw/2026-09-08T0100Z_ablation_ablation_digits/` and `…_regraded/`.
Tables in `results/tables/`, five figures in `results/figures/`. 135 tests pass.
